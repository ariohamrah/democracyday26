# Signal-source audit

Research cutoff: 2026-11-02T20:13:00-08:00. Later election results are excluded. These notes distinguish verified source availability from features actually included in the fitted model; inspect the generated model and validation report for inclusion.

## Polls

The public [FiftyPlusOne generic-ballot page](https://fiftyplusone.news/polls/generic-ballot/generic-ballot) displays November 2, 2026 averages of 52.7% Democratic and 44.1% Republican. Its [approval page](https://fiftyplusone.news/polls/approval/president) displays 35.4% approve and 61.5% disapprove. These four facts are transcribed in `national_public_snapshots.csv`, with date-level publication precision. They are model averages, not independent surveys. Do not combine these with overlapping underlying polls as independent evidence.

The [FiftyPlusOne download page](https://fiftyplusone.news/downloads) requires paid API access. No paid API was accessed or paywall bypassed. Publicly displayed aggregate facts do not imply permission to redistribute its full database.

FiveThirtyEight's public GitHub [raw poll archive](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/blob/master/pollster-ratings/raw_polls.csv) is a usable historical source (the real filename uses an underscore, although its README uses a hyphen). Its [codebook](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/blob/master/pollster-ratings/README.md) documents median field date, election date, sample size, candidates, party, poll percentages and partisan sponsor flag. The archive contains retrospective election outcomes and retrospective pollster statistics: never use actual-result columns, future pollster grades, or final election errors as pre-election predictors. Candidate ordering is defined using eventual top-two finishers; restrict historical fitting to ordinary D/R contests and disclose that selection limitation. Sample sizes can be imputed by the source. Median field date is not publication date, so this archive alone does not certify exact historical publication-time reconstruction.

[Historical generic model averages](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/fivethirtyeight/data/master/congress-generic-ballot/generic_topline_historical.csv) cover 1995–2016 but carry September 2020 calculation timestamps. They are retrospective reconstructions, not proof of original forecast-date availability. [Old-model averages](https://github.com/fivethirtyeight/data/tree/master/polls/old_model) include generic-ballot averages and Biden approval previously published before June 28, 2023. Prefer raw poll observations where available; do not call reconstructed averages leak-free snapshots without further checking.

## FEC

The [official all-candidates file description](https://www.fec.gov/campaign-finance-data/all-candidates-file-description/) documents candidate-level receipts, cash-on-hand and coverage end date. The 2026 bulk file is https://www.fec.gov/files/bulk-downloads/2026/weball26.zip . It is pipe-delimited with 30 fields; positions 1/2 identify candidate, 3 incumbency, 5 party, 6 receipts, 7 transfers from authorized committees, 9 transfers to authorized committees, 11 ending cash, 19 state, 20 district and 28 coverage end date. Transfers between authorized committees can double-count financial activity, per FEC. Coverage dates vary between candidates. A coverage date before cutoff does not prove the filing or amendment was available then. Historical final-cycle summaries must not be used as pre-election features; proper reconstruction needs filing receipt timestamps and amendment handling. No authenticated API access was required for the bulk-source discovery.

## Demographics

The [Census 2024 ACS API](https://api.census.gov/data/2024/acs.html) supplies district and state education, income and age statistics. [Census district documentation](https://www2.census.gov/data/api-documentation/how-to-access-data-for-congressional-districts.pdf) explicitly identifies congressional vintage. Do not join a 118th-Congress district demographic estimate directly to a different 2026 district merely because the district number matches. Changed districts require a geographic crosswalk and uncertainty for allocation, or missing values. A demographic source being available does not mean this model used it.

## Kalshi and scoring odds

The [official candlestick documentation](https://docs.kalshi.com/api-reference/market/get-market-candlesticks) gives `/series/{series_ticker}/markets/{ticker}/candlesticks`, with `start_ts`, `end_ts` and `period_interval` of 1, 60 or 1440 minutes. Candles have separate yes bid, yes ask, traded-price OHLC, volume and open interest. Historic settled markets use `/historical/markets/{ticker}/candlesticks`. Null traded prices are valid and must remain missing. The optional prior-price synthetic candle is not an observation at the requested time.

November 1 has no organizer-specified instant, timezone or price convention. Any recovered bid/ask midpoint or last-trade snapshot must therefore be labeled provisional, with the chosen UTC time, candle interval and precise price definition. Do not use current odds as November 1 prices. Contract outcomes may name a party being sworn in even when the UI displays a candidate name; verify official settlement rules before mapping each outcome. The Texas Senate event ticker discovered in indexed sources is `SENATETX-26`; obtaining the official event response is needed to verify market tickers and rules. Secondary odds sites and November 2 news are not official November 1 scoring snapshots.

Implemented organizer import fields: `race_id,outcome,contract_type,probability,snapshot_at,snapshot_timezone,price_definition,source_url,status,market_ticker`. Keep `q` separate. Missing `p` means scoring choice unresolved. Reject out-of-range values. Treat `p=0` as undefined/infinite reward requiring organizer clarification, not silent clipping; `p=1` yields zero reward. Derive complementary probabilities only when the contract outcomes are genuinely exhaustive and exclusive, not merely because two quotes are displayed. The market-trusting binary benchmark selects the underdog for strictly interior unequal complementary probabilities; at a tie both choices have equal expected score. Historical quotes and their derived selections are excluded from publication because redistribution permission was not established; see `market_data_publication.md`.

## Implemented polling processing and actual source gaps

`forecast/polling.py` normalizes the saved historical archive and the 2026 Election Wrld/VoteHub snapshot. It explicitly ignores all election-actual and retrospective pollster-grade columns. Local polls must match both modeled candidate names (with explicitly supplied aliases), preventing old primary matchups or the wrong Senate special contest from being attached. Polls use a 45-day local or 30-day generic window, a 21-day exponential recency scale, square-root sample-size weights capped at 2,000 respondents, and half weight for partisan surveys. These aggregation choices are fixed heuristics; downstream feature coefficients are learned. Missing sample sizes receive 600 only for aggregation weighting and remain missing in source records. Each firm/field-end-date contributes at most one question, preferring LV, then RV, then adults, then larger sample size. This limits duplicated question/population observations but cannot identify all overlapping samples across differing date windows.

Historical publication timestamps are unavailable. A three-day lag after the median field date guards against immediate publication leakage but does not certify an exact historical as-of dataset. Historical generic observations are available for 2006–2022; 2024 is deliberately missing. The historical dataset's selection of eventual top-two candidates and possible late-added surveys remain reconstruction limitations. Current eligibility requires both field end and aggregator-added date by the cutoff. Date-only November 2 entries have unknown intraday availability. Current source polling can include RCV first preferences; a binary normalized poll does not estimate transfer preferences or prove which two candidates advance.

The downloaded current generic-ballot snapshot has only **one** eligible final-30-day observation: Pew, fielded September 28–October 4, sample 3,917 RV, D44/R36, added October 9. The resulting raw generic margin is D+8.0, not a fabricated current average. This sparse recent generic coverage should be visible alongside the separately captured FiftyPlusOne D+8.6 average. Local Senate coverage is more recent; for example the TX principal matchup has 22 retained polls in the implemented 45-day window before November 2.

## Collected FEC audit

`scripts/normalize_fec.py` converts the saved official ZIP to `fec_candidates_2026.csv` (4,368 records) and `fec_nominee_coverage.csv`. Conservative name/state/office matching found 853 unique matches, 19 ambiguous cases, and 409 missing matches among 1,281 audited candidate rows. The matching does not verify committee lineage, and identical names in different district registrations are not silently resolved. `fec_audit.json` records the counts. Financial amounts are audit-only: filing-receipt/amendment timestamps and comparable historical snapshots were not obtained, so no financing coefficient is claimed. Demographics likewise remain excluded pending geographically compatible, dated historical coverage.
